# The 28 selected LSA gameplay experiences — Phase 1 draft

> **2026-10-18 | Status: proposed descriptions; not player-approved specifications.**
>
> These 28 IDs and original names come from the player's selection from 100 brainstormed ideas. Numbering is deliberately preserved. The remaining wording below is an initial interpretation to help the player refine the intended *fun*, not an engineering commitment or proof of feasibility. “First fun slice” is deliberately a small but real gameplay loop, never a fake showcase or mere dialogue.

## The 28 experience cards

### #1 — NPCs that actually understand what you're doing

- **The player moment:** You're tailing a car, and Nathan quietly says he thinks the driver spotted you. You didn't tell him you're tailing anybody.
- **Dream experience:** Nathan infers goals, tactics, and shifts in player intent from multiple grounded observations rather than treating each crash, gunshot, or movement as an isolated event.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** During one chosen scenario (e.g., following a target car), Nathan differentiates tailing from ordinary driving, comments only when sufficiently confident, and notices when the situation changes.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** The feeling that the NPC understands the situation, not a stream of generic event reactions. It must distinguish observed facts from uncertain interpretations.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can a player tell what Nathan understood without the system inventing unseen facts?

### #3 — AI-generated adventures

- **The player moment:** Nathan spontaneously suggests a weird place to visit, gets the two of you there, and an unplanned encounter becomes an adventure you actually play.
- **Dream experience:** Character-led open-ended outings with destinations, companions, setbacks, discoveries, and consequences that react to the player's decisions.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One proposed excursion with a verifiable destination, two playable beats, at least one meaningful player choice, and an honest completion or abandonment state.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Feels like a friend organizing an outing, not an anonymous quest board. Different from #23's mission structure and #72's on-demand genre experiences.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Would a player want to go on another adventure even if the dialogue were muted?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #6 — AI that can improvise entire action scenes

- **The player moment:** A quiet drive turns into a desperate rescue or chase, and Nathan improvises instead of waiting for a pre-scripted sequence.
- **Dream experience:** Real-time orchestration of chases, rescues, confrontations, evasions, and other set pieces using actors and actual game mechanics.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One small but dynamic chase/rescue scenario with changing circumstances, player interference, multiple plausible outcomes, and safe cancellation.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Action is unscripted enough to surprise, but never fake physical events; gameplay retains player control. Separate from #14 cinematic presentation.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Did the scene create an exciting playable decision rather than merely more NPC chatter?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #7 — NPCs that can see the game like you do

- **The player moment:** Point Nathan toward a sign, wreck, or doorway and have him correctly recognize the visible thing without someone hand-authoring a detector for it.
- **Dream experience:** Visual grounding from rendered gameplay or other reliable visual inputs, recognizing previously unmodeled scene details and making them useful to interaction.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Ask about one clearly visible object/location not already represented in LSA's structured events; answer with calibrated confidence and location context.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Actual visual interpretation, not a collection of hardcoded labels; no claiming to see outside the sampled frame or camera.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does the response demonstrably depend on visual evidence the game exposed?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #12 — NPCs that can learn new physical abilities

- **The player moment:** Ask Nathan to do something neither of you has ever tried, and watch him learn a reusable way to make it happen.
- **Dream experience:** Agents discover and refine action sequences from approved primitives, physical feedback, failure, and successful demonstrations.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Learn one unfamiliar two- or three-step task in a controlled scene; replay it successfully in a comparable scene while admitting when conditions differ.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Learning through verified world results, not merely generating code or a plan. #38 is discovery of primitives; this is learning to use them.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can we show measurable improvement without manual addition of a special-case command?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #13 — Spontaneous humor that actually develops

- **The player moment:** Days after an absurd crash, Nathan lands a perfectly timed callback that both of you recognize as an inside joke.
- **Dream experience:** Contextual humor with recurring bits, memory-backed jokes, playful teasing, and sensitivity to whether the joke is welcome or worn out.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One recurring, grounded joke from a real shared event that reappears appropriately during later gameplay and stops when dismissed.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Timing, personality and callbacks matter more than maximum joke frequency; avoid a random-quips machine.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does it feel like an earned joke between two people?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #14 — AI-directed cinematic moments

- **The player moment:** A chaotic shootout ends with a perfect dramatic glance, camera angle and musical sting—without freezing you into a cutscene.
- **Dream experience:** Context-sensitive in-world staging, optional camera framing, movement, dialogue timing, and audio emphasis around meaningful moments.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One brief opt-in cinematic flourish triggered by a verified moment, respecting player camera and controls, abortable instantly.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Improves presentation without substituting spectacle for action; distinct from #6's playable scene director.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does it make a moment memorable while preserving control and intelligibility?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #15 — NPCs can negotiate anything

- **The player moment:** You talk a guard into helping, promise a favor, and later the guard remembers whether you honored the bargain.
- **Dream experience:** Open-ended social persuasion mapped to bounded, verifiable agreements, concessions, refusals, and physical consequences.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Negotiate one nontrivial exchange with a character; they can counteroffer or refuse, execute the agreed action, and remember outstanding obligation.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** The bargain changes game state, not just dialogue. Character goals and leverage should matter.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Would the same encounter have played differently if the player negotiated differently?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #20 — An AI Game Master that invents gameplay

- **The player moment:** Nothing interesting is happening; the world quietly presents an opportunity that grows into a story because of what you choose.
- **Dream experience:** An overarching gameplay intelligence invents, introduces, observes, and revises interesting situations across LSA systems without forcing a plot.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One repeatable framework that selects from or composes two different interactive event shapes, tracks genuine outcomes, and yields control to normal play.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** The GM creates opportunities rather than narrating things that didn't happen. It is infrastructure plus a distinct emergent gameplay experience.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does it reliably create more playable situations rather than interrupting normal sandbox freedom?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #23 — Fully improvised missions

- **The player moment:** Tell Nathan, 'Let's pull off a job,' then collaborate on a plan with objectives, a getaway, unexpected complications and a clear outcome.
- **Dream experience:** Executable multi-stage missions built from actual locations, props, NPC capabilities and state checks, improvising when player actions disrupt the plan.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Generate a modest two-to-four-stage job with explicit success/failure, alternative routes, and actual physical progress measurements.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Player-led, intentional mission planning; different from #3 companion outings and #20 world-level opportunity orchestration.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Could the mission be completed, failed, or creatively rerouted through normal GTA play?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #25 — Characters understand how you sound, not just what you say

- **The player moment:** Say 'you idiot' laughing after a stunt and Nathan laughs; say the same words furious and he reacts differently.
- **Dream experience:** Ground conversational interpretation in vocal tone, sarcasm, laughter, emphasis and frustration, with uncertainty and personality-specific responses.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Distinguish two real recorded deliveries of the same phrase, adjust the response naturally, and allow the player to correct a misread.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Must work on paralinguistic evidence, not sentiment analysis of transcript text alone.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can the reaction be explained by what the player sounded like?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #28 — AI improvises physical interactions with the environment

- **The player moment:** Nathan notices a movable car could block pursuers and uses it in a way we never implemented as a named mission action.
- **Dream experience:** Object affordances, spatial reasoning and composable primitives let NPCs use scenery, vehicles and props to solve problems creatively.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One scenario where two distinct world objects provide alternative workable solutions, with state checks after execution.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Solves problems with actual objects; distinguish from #12 learning an ability and #39 predicting consequences.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does Nathan manipulate the world in a surprising but physically grounded way?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #30 — NPCs possess genuine spatial memory

- **The player moment:** Nathan leads you back to where you left a getaway car hours ago, even after you have driven across the city.
- **Dream experience:** Persistent, uncertainty-aware knowledge of landmarks, routes, object locations, visited places, and where events actually occurred.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Park a car, leave the area, return later and ask Nathan to navigate to its last verified location; he qualifies if the car could have moved.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Remembered geography and source-time certainty—not omniscient access to the newest game state.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can the character recall useful places across meaningful gameplay transitions?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #32 — NPCs can change their minds

- **The player moment:** Nathan agrees to drive through a risky area, discovers a blockade, argues for another route, and adapts without losing the original goal.
- **Dream experience:** Continuous, bounded goal reassessment as fresh evidence, player orders, physical constraints and risks change.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One multi-step task where new information forces a reasoned choice among continuing, rerouting, requesting permission, or aborting.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Real reconsideration, not random wavering or silent disregard of player orders.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does adaptation make a visible, sensible difference to the plan?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #38 — AI discovers GTA mechanics we haven't programmed

- **The player moment:** An experimental agent discovers that two obscure actions can be combined into a useful new trick, proves it works, then teaches Nathan.
- **Dream experience:** Safe, controlled exploration of GTA native interactions and mod APIs to identify undocumented or underused abilities.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Discover or validate one previously unregistered interaction in an isolated test scene; produce a reproducible action recipe and limitations.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Empirical mechanic discovery, not invented API signatures or uncontrolled trial and error during normal play.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can a second run reproduce the result without a developer hand-authoring the exact behavior?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #39 — NPCs understand and anticipate real-world physics

- **The player moment:** Nathan sees a car lose control and warns you or dives clear before the collision, not afterward.
- **Dream experience:** Short-horizon prediction of trajectories, impact risks, falling objects and changing physical hazards tied to the real GTA simulation.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Detect one moving-object hazard, predict where it may intersect the actor, and take a useful defensive action within a believable reaction window.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Anticipatory interaction, not another retrospective crash event notification.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can we measure a correct prediction and corresponding physical response?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #40 — LSA can invent entirely new gameplay mechanics

- **The player moment:** Ask for a new kind of challenge and LSA devises a bounded mechanic, proves its rules, and lets you play it.
- **Dream experience:** Research into AI-assisted creation and safe validation of genuinely new gameplay systems, interactions or mods beyond a fixed command catalog.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** An AI produces a simple, isolated new rule-based activity from a supported extension interface; tests, review gates and manual enablement precede gameplay.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Mechanic invention beyond quest content generation (#72). Do not imply arbitrary runtime code is safe or feasible.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does it alter what can be played, rather than merely reskinning an existing event?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #41 — Los Santos physically remembers everything you've done

- **The player moment:** Return to the same street after a previous shootout and recognize the damage and aftermath you left behind.
- **Dream experience:** Persistent world scars, wreckage, object alterations, repairs, visual traces and histories that survive streaming and sessions where possible.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One chosen location retains a visible, inspectable change between sessions with cleanup/repair controls and unambiguous save lineage.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** The world remembers physically, not solely through Nathan saying he remembers it.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can the player recognize a personal history by looking at the environment?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

- **Player clarification from first physics interview (2026-10-18):** For the destruction project specifically, environmental changes should **persist across GTA sessions** rather than resetting when the player leaves/restarts. This reinforces the original #41 goal, without settling implementation or save-lineage handling.

### #45 — AI-generated explorable interiors

- **The player moment:** Walk up to an ordinary locked storefront, enter it, and discover a coherent shop full of usable objects and NPC activity.
- **Dream experience:** Generation or assembly of accessible layouts, furnishings, collisions, navigation surfaces, and scene content consistent with external buildings.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One previously decorative building leads to a reproducible, furnished, explorable interior with working entry/exit and reliable basic collision.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Genuinely explorable gameplay space; not an image backdrop or disconnected teleport room pretending to match the exterior.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does it withstand player exploration, and can NPCs move through it?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #72 — AI-generated immersive experiences inside GTA

- **The player moment:** Ask Nathan for a horror experience and a playable, atmospheric mystery unfolds in an actual Los Santos location.
- **Dream experience:** On-demand game-within-a-game experiences, with genre-specific mechanics, puzzles, pacing, actors and win/loss conditions.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One short opt-in experience (e.g., horror investigation) created from existing mechanics and a location, with a satisfying end and no hardcoded quest narrative.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Different from #23: player requests a genre experience, not necessarily a mission; different from #40: composes mechanics rather than inventing executable ones.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Would this be fun as a small standalone playable session?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #81 — Actual destructible buildings and environments

- **The player moment:** Blow an opening through a wall, alter your route, and watch the resulting debris affect the world.
- **Dream experience:** Localized structural damage, holes, collapses and changed collision/navigation; potentially expanding from small props to whole buildings.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One isolated destructible wall/section with believable visuals, safe debris, and actual passage/collision changes.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Destruction must change gameplay geometry, not merely display an explosion or swap a cosmetic texture.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can a player exploit the destroyed structure as a new route or cover state?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

- **Player clarification (2026-10-18, authoritative dream):** Wants essentially **everything destructible**, including bullet-made holes, damaged floors, collapsible whole buildings **and bridges**. A single destructible wall is only a candidate experimental slice—not the accepted final scale.

### #82 — BeamNG-level vehicle destruction

- **The player moment:** Smash the front suspension in a pursuit and feel the car pull sideways while damaged parts hang off.
- **Dream experience:** Physically meaningful deformation, component failures and handling consequences well beyond a generic vehicle health bar.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One experimental vehicle with localized wheel/suspension damage that visibly changes behavior and remains drivable or becomes unusable as appropriate.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Keep mechanical consequences and crunchy destruction separate from #98's arcade stunt spectacle; don't claim true BeamNG fidelity yet.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Would two differently damaged cars drive meaningfully differently?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

- **Player clarification (2026-10-18):** Strong preference for **BeamNG-style mechanical realism rather than Burnout arcade exaggeration**. This remains an interesting separate feature, but is secondary to the first Astra ballistic/structural-destruction investigation.

### #90 — Max Payne-style cinematic gunfights

- **The player moment:** Dive through a doorway firing in slow motion, hit the floor, and scramble into cover while enemies react dynamically.
- **Dream experience:** Fluid combat moves, bullet time, dives, disarms, reactive animation/ragdolls and tactical enemies.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One controllable directional shoot-dodge interaction with precise movement, shooting, impact, and recovery plus a small reactive enemy test.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** The playable feel and responsive controls matter; not just added slow-motion visuals or AI dialogue.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Does the player deliberately choose and enjoy the mechanic repeatedly?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

- **Player clarification (2026-10-18):** Max Payne-style bullet-time dives, realistic ragdolls and dynamic hit reactions all appeal, but **realistic bullet penetration through walls is especially important**, and must interact with physically destructible openings. Gunplay and destruction are linked, not isolated visual features.

### #93 — Physics-based demolition missions

- **The player moment:** Study a warehouse, choose supports, plant charges, and watch whether your plan actually brings it down.
- **Dream experience:** Playable demolition contracts or sandbox challenges with structural objectives, budgets, equipment and surprising physical outcomes.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One challenge built around #81's verified destructible structure with target zones, success/failure scoring, and multiple approaches.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Distinct mode with mastery and replayability; shares destruction implementation with #81, not the whole gameplay design.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can players improve their demolition strategy across attempts?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

- **Player clarification (2026-10-18):** Physically plausible collapse is desired, not merely spectacular canned destruction. Whether demolition contracts become a formal game mode is **not yet decided**; free-form structural destruction is the proven priority.

### #94 — A real tactical AI squad

- **The player moment:** Tell Nathan to take the roof, another ally to watch the exit, then breach while they cover each other.
- **Dream experience:** Multi-NPC tactical roles, coordination, line of sight, cover, suppressive fire, communication, and plan adaptation from real outcomes.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** Two AI companions execute different roles in one small encounter; player can issue natural orders, change them, and verify results.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Feels like cooperative tactics, not several independent followers shooting at random.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can coordinated allies reliably do something a single follower cannot?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

### #95 — Real fire propagation and environmental disasters

- **The player moment:** A car explodes near dry brush; wind pushes fire across a field while people flee and responders arrive.
- **Dream experience:** Local propagation through fuel, proximity and environmental conditions with containment, secondary hazards and playable emergencies.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One bounded patch of vegetation/props spreads fire under explicit fuel and wind rules and can be extinguished or contained.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Cascading, interactable risk; fire must actually spread and alter options, not only increase particle effects.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Can the player predict, exploit or stop the spread through gameplay?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

- **Player decision (2026-10-18):** **Withdrawn from the current Physics/Astra investigation**: spreading fire and environmental disasters are considered too ambitious for this focus. Historical idea preserved; no active research requirements.

### #98 — Burnout-style vehicle combat and stunt physics

- **The player moment:** Smash a rival into roadside scenery, earn a spectacular takedown, and chain the chaos into another stunt.
- **Dream experience:** Arcade vehicle takedowns, exaggerated stunt systems, spectacular crashes, destructible scenery and replayable competitive challenges.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One seamless high-speed takedown mode with physical crash detection, rewarding feedback and replay value.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** Intentionally exciting arcade feel; don't dissolve it into #82's simulation-first vehicle engineering.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Is it immediately thrilling even without a quest or narrative?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_

- **Player clarification (2026-10-18):** Burnout-style exaggeration is **not the vehicle-physics priority**; BeamNG-like realism takes precedence. Keep the original stunt idea in the favorites archive, but do not include arcade stunts as a first Astra destruction requirement.

### #100 — A fully dynamic destruction-and-physics playground

- **The player moment:** Use cranes, vehicles, props, buildings and wreckage to solve absurd problems while Nathan experiments alongside you.
- **Dream experience:** A coherent sandbox combining environmental destruction, vehicle damage, interactive objects, fire and NPC physical reasoning.
- **First genuinely fun playable slice:** One small test arena integrating at least two independent physics systems with consequences another system (or Nathan) can detect and use.
- **Irreplaceable flair / scope boundary:** An integration vision, not an excuse to build a single monolithic physics engine; preserve each system's gameplay identity.
- **Player-experience acceptance question:** Do combined systems create genuinely new possibilities that neither enables alone?
- **Owner review:** ☐ Approve ☐ Revise ☐ Reject/park; notes: _TBD_


- **Player clarification (2026-10-18, supersedes generic playground description):** Wants a **realistic ballistic and structural-destruction world**, not primarily a broad physics sandbox. Bullets penetrate walls and knock chunks loose; explosions remove larger sections; authentic structural failures collapse floors, buildings and bridges; the modified world persists. Nathan's integration and generic object-manipulation systems are **not initial requirements**. See [complete vision interview](ballistics-destruction-vision-20261018.md).

## Cross-cutting clarification questions (not blockers to archiving this draft)
- Which ideas should emerge organically, and which should be explicitly toggled/started by the player?
- Which features should work with *any* NPC and which should be exclusive to promoted companions such as Nathan?
- What should persist after restarting GTA, loading a different save, or changing character?
- What quality or performance compromises are acceptable for the first fun version, especially simulation and live AI cost?
- When NPC autonomy conflicts with the player's immediate orders, what kinds of interruption or veto should always win?

## Preservation rule
Each numbered experience remains an independent player-facing goal even if research shows that two or more can reuse the same technical services. See [provisional programs](provisional-programs.md).
